About the Author

K. R. Miller is a researcher, analyst, and advocate whose work focuses on the intersection of race, law, and systemic power in the United States. Drawing on extensive documentary research and a deep engagement with the historical record, Miller has dedicated years to tracing the patterns of racial control across American history. The Infinity Loop: Race, Power, and the Machinery of Control represents the culmination of this research, synthesizing 160 years of evidence into a comprehensive analysis of how systems of surveillance, criminalization, extraction, and destabilization have operated and adapted across American history. Miller's approach combines scholarly rigor with accessible writing, creating work that speaks to multiple audiences: academics seeking comprehensive synthesis, activists and organizers needing analytical frameworks, policymakers requiring evidence-based documentation, and general readers seeking to understand persistent racial inequalities. This book emerges from a commitment to documentation as a form of resistance, to the belief that naming systems with precision is the first step toward dismantling them, and to the conviction that understanding history is essential to building a more just future. Miller's work has been informed by the scholarship of critical race theorists, abolition scholars, historians, sociologists, and legal analysts, as well as by the wisdom of community organizers, activists, and the lived experiences of those who have resisted oppression across generations. Beyond academic research, Miller has been involved in community-based organizing efforts, legal advocacy work, and public education initiatives aimed at exposing and challenging systemic racism. The Infinity Loop is offered as a tool for understanding and action, a contribution to the long struggle for freedom and justice.
