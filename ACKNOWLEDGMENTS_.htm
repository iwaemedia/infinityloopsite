Acknowledgments

This book would not exist without the work of countless scholars, activists, journalists, and community members who have documented, analyzed, and resisted the systems of control examined in these pages.

I am deeply indebted to the historians who have painstakingly reconstructed the documentary record of racial oppression, from Ralph Ginzburg’s compilation of lynching accounts to the contemporary scholars whose peer-reviewed research provides the empirical foundation for this analysis.

I owe a profound debt to the legal scholars and civil rights attorneys whose litigation has forced the system to reveal itself, from Thurgood Marshall’s strategic dismantling of Jim Crow to the contemporary advocates using public records requests and impact litigation to expose algorithmic bias and surveillance overreach.

The journalists and investigative reporters whose work appears throughout this book, particularly those at ProPublica, The Marshall Project, and major newspapers who have dedicated careers to documenting injustice, deserve recognition for their essential contributions to the historical record.

I am grateful to the abolition scholars and movement intellectuals, from Angela Davis to Ruth Wilson Gilmore to Mariame Kaba, whose frameworks have shaped contemporary understanding of carceral systems and provided the theoretical foundation for imagining their dismantlement.

The community organizers, activists, and ordinary people who have resisted the Loop across generations, often at great personal cost, are the true authors of the resistance documented in Chapter 10. Their courage, creativity, and persistence in the face of overwhelming force is the foundation upon which any future liberation must be built. To the families of those wrongfully convicted, eliminated, or harmed by the systems documented here, I hope this work serves as some form of tribute and as evidence in the ongoing struggle for justice.

To those currently incarcerated, under surveillance, or navigating the carceral state, this book is offered as documentation that your experience is not individual failing but systemic design, and that the fight for your freedom continues.

I acknowledge the limitations of any single work to capture the full scope of either oppression or resistance. This book is one contribution to a much larger collective project of documentation, analysis, and transformation.

Finally, to the generations who will continue this work, the young organizers and scholars and community members who will write the next chapters, this book is offered as both evidence and encouragement. The Loop is powerful, but it is not eternal. What was built can be dismantled. What was written can be rewritten. The work continues.
